## Weekly structure

Sunday school dives deep into study and theology. Wednesday's focus is more comprehensive discipleship. Prayer is the common thread connecting both groups.

On Wednesday, high school works through the Journey curriculum — the sections are **Truth, Equipping, Accountability, Mission** and **Supplication**. We try to hit each one for a well-rounded, formative discipleship time.

Jr high takes one passage or concept from the same Journey unit and works through a few questions together in ten minutes, then jumps to fellowship and games.

Sunday and Wednesday attendance barely overlaps, so each night stands on its own — questions are always about what's taught that night.

## Big idea

Glory was what you're looking for. Grace was how you're related to God. Truth is the path between them — and it's a path you walk, not one you hear about. Jesus ties four things together in John 8: abide in my word → you're truly my disciples → you'll know the truth → the truth sets you free. Abiding comes first. Tonight they get the two tools that make abiding possible on a Tuesday when nobody's watching.

## Condensed teacher study material

*From Journey pp. 70–71 and Appendix A, pp. 386–388.*

**Truth is the third word (p. 70).** The Journey's arc so far: we embrace **glory** by experiencing Christ in daily personal worship; we embrace **grace** by living in the freedom the cross bought. Now **truth** — God's path to full glory.

**John 8:31–32, the memory verse.** Abide in my word → truly my disciples → know the truth → the truth sets you free. The order matters and it starts with abiding.

**Journey names the obstacle plainly (p. 70).** Studying truth forces the question of whether absolute truth exists at all. Naturalistic philosophy says truth, morality and meaning are created by each person. That's the water your students swim in, and it's worth naming rather than assuming.

**The TEAMS frame (Appendix A, p. 386).** Worth showing once, because it explains why Wednesday is shaped the way it is:

- **Truth** — what God has revealed for His people to know, understand and obey.
- **Equipping** — massaging God's truth into life so it becomes understandable and usable.
- **Accountability** — asking the hard questions to encourage living fully for Christ.
- **Mission** — engaging the lost world to impart the gospel in word and deed.
- **Supplication** — conversation with God to express dependence on Him.

Every Wednesday night runs this shape. Tonight you can say so out loud.

**PRAISE — the reading tool (Appendix A, p. 387).**

- **P** — Pray first
- **R** — Read the text
- **A** — Ask questions
- **I** — Interpret in context
- **S** — Summarize applications
- **E** — Engage with God

**Five Targets of Prayer (Appendix A, p. 388).** The Lord's Prayer, used as an order of operations.

1. **God's honor** — acknowledging His worth. *"Hallowed be your name."* Start with God Himself: His greatness, His rule over all things, His fatherly love. Who He is and what He's like.
2. **God's kingdom** — acknowledging His priority. *"Your kingdom come, your will be done."* His rule over you and the world. Invite Him into what you just read and into specific situations, family, friends, your prayer list.
3. **God's provision** — acknowledging His trustworthiness. *"Give us this day our daily bread."* His ability and willingness to meet needs. Blessing on work and home; contentment with what He provides, great or small.
4. **God's forgiveness** — acknowledging your repentance. *"Forgive us our debts."* Confess specifically. Ask forgiveness for Christ's sake, and rejoice that it's promised. Pray that Christ's righteousness shows in your life.
5. **God's power** — acknowledging your dependence. *"Deliver us from the evil one."* His promise to deliver from temptation. Pour out troubles, bring requests, keep a list — tracking answers grows faith.

**From this week on**, the weekly one practice usually uses one of these two tools. That's the payoff for tonight.
