# High School Study Guide · Week 2 — Grace

**Live in the room.** Read the leader prep doc beforehand if you can; this sheet works without it.

**Sep 23 · Black Rock · 30 min after the walk**

One leader per five students. Six or more, split boy/girl. Five or fewer, stay whole.

**Bring:** handouts, pens.

**4 · 5 · 10 · 4 · 2 · 5.** Not into the handout by minute ten means you're behind. Take the time back from section 3, never from 4, 5 or 6.

---

## 1 · Land the room — 4 min

Drinks in hand, phones down, everyone sitting. Don't start over the ordering.

First study of the term, so nothing to check back on. Open cold:

> Who's the one person you most want to be impressed with you? Not like — impressed. What do you do differently around them?

Coach, dad, a girl, a group chat. Take every answer. Don't connect it to anything yet.

If it's flat: *what's something you've done purely so somebody would notice?*

*From week 3 on this slot is always "last week's one practice — what'd you notice?" Never skip it.*

---

## 2 · Truth — 5 min

Point back at what they just said.

> Everybody here has somebody they perform for. Do you do that with God?

Draw it on a napkin:

**What I do for God** → self-righteousness
**What God has done for me** → grace

Every religion picks one of those two. Read 2 Corinthians 5:21 and name it: the great swap. Your sin to Him, His righteousness to you.

**Ask:** which one feels more natural?

Performance. Let them say it.

Then stop.

---

## 3 · Equipping — 10 min

Read **Ephesians 2:1–13** together, split by verse.

Handout. Q1 warms up; **Q2 and Q3 are the night.**

- Ask, then wait. Count to ten.
- Sunday-school answer: *"what does that look like on a Tuesday?"*
- Follow one thread deep rather than one answer from everyone.
- Your own answer goes in the middle, and it should cost you something.

**If it stalls,** tell them this — don't cite it, just tell it:

> A husband has already decided to bail on a date with his wife for a selfish reason. Before he tells her, he buys her flowers and a gift. She's flattered — until she finds out why. Then she's disgusted.
>
> That's performance. Giving God the things He loves, with the wrong motive underneath.

Then re-ask Q3.

**Cut from here if you're behind.** Drop Q1, go to Q2.

---

## 4 · Accountability — 4 min

They've written about where they run on performance. Don't ask it a third time. Ask what it costs:

> If there's nothing left to earn — what would you stop doing this week?

Everyone answers, including you. One sentence each.

When someone says something real, respond with a question about it, not a comment. *"How long has that been going on?" "Who else knows that?"* No advice, no verse.

**Watch for:** the kid who makes it somebody else's problem, and the kid who answers fast to move past it. *"Say more"* handles both.

---

## 5 · Mission — 2 min

> One name each. Somebody wearing themselves out trying to be good enough.

No plan, no commitment. Say the name. We'll ask again next week.

---

## 6 · Supplication — 5 min

Pray for each other by name, for what they said in section 4. Then the mission names.

Students pray if they want to. Nobody's called on. If nobody volunteers, you pray for each of them by name.

Night ends here. High school does not walk back.

---

## 7 · One practice — as they stand up

> This week, notice when you do something for God to get Him to like you. Church, being nice, reading your Bible, *not* doing something. Don't try to stop. Just catch the motive.

Nothing to write, nothing to turn in.

**Next week I open by asking what you noticed.** Then do it.

---

## Notes

- "Grace" is a word they'll nod at. Force the contrast — *what I do* vs. *what He did* — and make them say which one they run on.
- Don't rush to Do-or-Done; that's next week. If someone asks "so what do I do?", tonight's answer is *nothing.* Let it sit.
- Leave church on time.
