# High School Leader Sheet · Week 2 — Grace

**Sep 23 · Black Rock · ~30 min after the walk**

One leader per five students. Six or more, split boy/girl. Five or fewer, stay whole.

**You are talking for about eight of these thirty minutes.** If you're talking more than that, the night has drifted into a lecture with drinks.

---

## 1 · Land the room — 5 min

Drinks in hand, phones down, everybody sitting before you start. Don't teach over the ordering.

First real week, so there's no prior practice to check. Use Sunday instead:

> Sunday we named the counterfeits — praise, power, pleasure. Did you catch yourself chasing one this week?

Take every answer. Don't improve on them. If someone says "not really," ask what their week actually looked like and let them find it.

*From week 3 on, this slot is always "last week's one practice — what'd you notice?" That question is what makes this discipleship instead of a talk. Never skip it, never rush it.*

---

## 2 · Truth — 5 min, and no longer

Two ways to relate to God. Draw it on a napkin if there's one:

**What I do for God** → self-righteousness
**What God has done for me** → grace

Every religion picks one. Say the memory verse — 2 Corinthians 5:21 — and name it: the great swap.

**Ask:** which one feels more natural?

The answer is performance, always. Let them say it rather than you.

Then stop. Everything else tonight comes out of them.

---

## 3 · Equipping — the talking — 10 min

Read **Ephesians 2:1–13** together, out loud, split by verse around the table. Then work the handout.

Questions 1 and 2 warm up the passage. **Questions 3 and 4 are the night** — plan to spend most of the ten minutes there.

**How to run it**

- Ask, then wait. Count to ten in your head. The first silence is where the real answer is being decided on.
- When someone gives the Sunday-school answer, don't correct it. Ask: *"what does that look like on a Tuesday?"*
- Follow one thread deep instead of collecting one answer from everyone. Depth beats coverage.
- Your own answer goes in the middle, not first, and it should cost you something.

**If it stalls**, the husband-and-wife picture from the study material lands hard with teenagers — the gifts given to buy her off, and her reaction when she learns why. Use it and re-ask question 4.

---

## 4 · Accountability — the honest turn — 5 min

This is the part that gets cut when the clock slips. Protect it instead.

> Where are you actually running on performance right now? Not in general — this week.

Go around. Everyone answers, including you. One sentence each is fine; nobody has to elaborate.

**Watch for:** the kid who converts it into someone else's problem, and the kid who answers fast and shallow to get past it. A gentle *"say more"* is enough for both.

**Don't fix anything anyone says tonight.** No advice, no verse volleyed back. Grace is the point — modeling it in how you receive what they say teaches more than the teaching did.

---

## 5 · Mission — 2 min

> One name each. Somebody you know who's exhausting themselves trying to be good enough.

No commitment attached, no plan. Just say the name out loud. We'll pray for them in a second, and we'll ask about them again.

---

## 6 · Supplication — 5 min

Pray for each other **by name**, out loud, for the specific thing they said in the accountability round. Then pray for the names from mission.

Students pray if they will. Nobody's forced. If it's the first week and nobody volunteers, you pray for each of them by name — that models it faster than explaining it.

This is where the night ends. High school does not walk back.

---

## 7 · One practice — before they stand up

> This week, notice when you do something for God to get Him to like you. Church, being nice, reading your Bible, *not* doing something. You're not trying to stop. Just catch the motive in the moment.

Nothing to write, nothing to turn in, nothing to lose.

Say it plainly: **next week I'm going to open by asking what you noticed.** Then actually do it.

---

## Watch-outs

- "Grace" is a word they'll nod at. Force the contrast — *what I do* vs. *what He did* — and make them say which one they actually run on.
- Don't rush to faith; it's next week. If someone asks "so what do I do?", the answer tonight is *nothing — that's the point.* Let it sit.
- Don't let the walk over eat the session. Leave on time.
