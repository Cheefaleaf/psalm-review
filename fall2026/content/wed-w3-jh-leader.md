# Jr High Study Guide · Week 3 — Faith

**Live in the room.** Read the leader prep document beforehand if you can; this guide works without it.

**Sep 30 · Main room · ~10 min, then games**

**The one thing:** every other religion says *do*. Jesus says *done*.

Last week landed "you can't earn it." This week answers the follow-up: then why be good?

---

## What you need to know

Last week: you can't earn God's love. This week answers the obvious follow-up — *then why be good?*

Paul says we're saved by faith, not works. James says faith without works is dead. They agree: God declares you righteous because of Christ, and a person who's actually received that starts to look different. The change is the *result*, never the price.

For jr high, the whole thing fits in one picture: how you treat someone who's already forgiven you.

---

## Run of it

**Two words — 2 min.** Write **DO** and **DONE** where they can see them.

> Every religion in the world is a to-do list. Be good enough, and God's happy with you. Christianity is the only one that says the list is already finished. Not by you.

**Read it — 2 min.** **Ephesians 2:8–9** — you read it, then a student. Same verse as last week on purpose. Ask what word in there means "you didn't do it." (*Gift.*)

**Ask it — 4 min.** The real question, the one they had last week:

> If you can't earn it and it's already done — why be good at all?

Take every answer. Somebody will say "so you don't get in trouble." Somebody will say "because God says so." Both are fine, don't correct them.

Then hand them the better one:

> Think about somebody who's already forgiven you for something big. Do you treat them worse after, or better?

**Conclusion — 2 min.** The punchline:

> You're not being good to get God to love you. You're being good because He already does.

Games.

---

## Notes

- "You don't have to be good" lands as permission. Get to the forgiven-friend picture before that hardens.
- "So I could do whatever I want?" — yes, technically. Then ask the friend question again. Don't add rules.
- Leave DO/DONE on the board through games.
