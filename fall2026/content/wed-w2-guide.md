## Big idea

There are only two ways to relate to God, and every religion picks one. **Performance** — what I do for God. **Grace** — what God has done for me. Performance feels natural and produces self-righteousness. Grace is the cross: God swapped our sin for Christ's righteousness. Sunday named the counterfeits we chase. Tonight names the deeper trap — chasing God's approval the same way.

## Condensed teacher study material

*From Journey pp. 46–50. Read the full pages; this is the spine.*

**Two ways to relate to God (p. 48).** Everyone who believes in God wants to be rightly related to Him — assured of His goodness now and when they die. There are two ways, and every religion follows one:

**1 · Performance — what I do for God.** Doing things (or not doing things) to gain God's approval. The determination it takes produces a real kind of goodness Journey calls **self-righteousness.** We say we hate self-righteousness, but what we actually hate is the arrogant kind — the Pharisee. Regular, humble self-righteous people we like fine. They make good neighbors.

**Jesus was repulsed by all of it (p. 48)** because He cares about motive. Journey's picture: a husband who's decided to break a date with his wife for selfish reasons, and tries to buy her off first with gifts. She's flattered until she learns the motive — then she's repulsed. Same with God: He loves church, giving, serving — but only when they come from love for Him, not from trying to get Him to like us. **Performance is giving God the things He loves with the wrong motive.**

**2 · Grace — what God has done for me (p. 49).** The cross. Memory verse: God made Christ to be sin so that in Him we might become the righteousness of God. Journey calls it **the great swap** — our sinfulness exchanged for Christ's righteousness. This is **declared righteousness**: God declares a person righteous the moment the swap happens.

**Justification (p. 49)** — *just-as-if-I'd-never-sinned.* Doesn't mean sinless. Means God pardons the penalty for all our sin. Colossians 2:13–14: the record of debt cancelled, nailed to the cross. We can still offend Him and need to confess to recover joy — but nothing alters His love, because our standing rests entirely on what Christ did.

**Does grace mean live however you want? (p. 49)** No and yes. No — a prayer doesn't cause the swap; like a wedding vow, it expresses a love that already exists. Yes — believers are free from sin's debt and now have the moral ability to do good *out of love* rather than to earn anything. (That newborn love is faith — next week.)

**The struggle is normal (p. 50).** Even born-again Christians default to performance. Journey's diagnostics: Do you look at past failures and wonder if they affect God's love? Do you go to church, give, or read your Bible because you're worried God's favor slips if you don't?
