# High School Leader Sheet · Week 3 — Do or Done

**Sep 30 · Black Rock · ~30 min after the walk**

One leader per five students. Six or more, split boy/girl.

You're talking for about eight of these thirty minutes.

---

## 1 · Land the room — 5 min

**This is the week the rhythm becomes real. Do not shortcut it.**

> Last week I asked you to notice when you do something for God to get Him to like you. What'd you notice?

Go around. Everyone answers, including you.

**What you'll get, and what to do with it:**

- *"I didn't really think about it."* Fine — ask what their week looked like and walk them into one moment. Don't let them off with nothing, don't shame them.
- *"All the time."* Ask for one specific instance. Specific is the whole game.
- *"I don't think I do that."* Ask about the moment right before church, or right after they've done something they're not proud of.

Whatever comes out here is tonight's raw material. Refer back to their own examples in section 3 — that's what makes this feel like one conversation instead of a series of lessons.

---

## 2 · Truth — 5 min, and no longer

Two words on a napkin: **DO** and **DONE**.

Every religion in the world spells the way to God *D-O* — here's the list, get it done. Christianity spells it *D-O-N-E*.

Then the objection, because a sharp kid will raise it and it's better coming from you:

> Paul says we're saved by faith, not works. James says faith without works is dead. So which is it?

Give the answer in one pass: Paul is talking about **declared** righteousness — Christ's record, credited to you. James is talking about **demonstrated** righteousness — what shows up in someone who actually got it. Declared comes first and causes the second. Reverse them and you're back to Do.

Stop there. The three beliefs come out in the handout, not from you.

---

## 3 · Equipping — the talking — 10 min

Read **Ephesians 2:1–13** together, split by verse. They read it last week; this time they're hunting.

Question 1 on the handout has them sort the passage into **we lost it all / He did it all / we get it all**. Do that one together, out loud, as a group — it's the only place tonight you work the text collectively.

Then questions 2 and 3, which are the night.

**How to run it**

- Ask, then wait. Ten seconds of silence is where the real answer gets decided on.
- Tie back to what they said in the opener. *"You said the thing about before church — which of the three is that?"*
- Follow one thread deep instead of collecting an answer from everyone.
- Your own answer goes in the middle, and it should cost you something.

**The gap is the point.** Most people don't reject any of the three outright — they shave one. Lost *most* of it. He did *most* of it. We get *most* of it. Whichever one a kid shaves is where their performance lives. Say that out loud once and let them find theirs.

---

## 4 · Accountability — the honest turn — 5 min

Journey's own accountability questions this week, lightly put:

> Which of the three do you actually have trouble believing about yourself?

And if the room can take it:

> Anyone been running the other direction — using "it's all grace" as cover for something?

That second one is James's concern and it's a fair question for a church kid. Ask it evenly, not as an accusation.

Everyone answers. **Don't fix anything anyone says.** No verse volleyed back, no advice. How you receive it teaches more than the teaching did.

---

## 5 · Mission — 2 min

> One name each. Same as last week if it's the same person — that's good, not lazy.

Ask whether anything happened with last week's name. Don't require it.

---

## 6 · Supplication — 5 min

Pray for each other by name, for the specific belief they said they struggle with. Then the names from mission.

Students pray if they will. Nobody's forced.

---

## 7 · One practice — before they stand up

> This week, catch yourself shaving one of the three. When you feel like you have to make something up to God, ask which one you stopped believing for a second — lost it all, He did it all, or we get it all.

Nothing to write. Say plainly: **next week I open by asking what you noticed.**

---

## Watch-outs

- If someone says "so it doesn't matter what I do" — that's James's exact worry and a good place to be. The answer isn't rules, it's love. Ask what they'd do for someone who'd already forgiven them everything.
- Don't turn the three beliefs into a quiz. They're a diagnostic, not a catechism.
- Week 4 installs PRAISE and the Five Targets. Don't preview it; just get tonight landed.
