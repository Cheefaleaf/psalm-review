## Big idea

Two words: **Do** or **Done**. Every religion on earth spells the way to God *D-O*. Christianity spells it *D-O-N-E*. Paul says we're justified by faith apart from performance; James says faith without works is dead. They aren't fighting. Paul is talking about **declared** righteousness — Christ's work, credited to you. James is talking about **demonstrated** righteousness — what a person who's actually received that will look like. Declared comes first and causes the second. Never the reverse.

## Condensed teacher study material

*From Journey pp. 50–52. Read the full pages; this is the spine.*

**Do or Done (p. 50).** Where does your spiritual pilgrimage begin? If you say *Do*, you may live an attractive, moral, religious life — and it will not earn you God's favor or love. For Paul and James both, rightly relating to God rests solely on what God has done.

**Paul and James, reconciled (p. 50).** Paul taught justification by faith apart from performance. James taught that faith without performance is useless. The resolution: Paul means **declared righteousness**, based on Christ's work on the cross alone. James means **demonstrated righteousness** — his point being that if we claim declared righteousness and our lives show nothing of it, we never really received it. Journey's diagram puts these as two sides: God at the top, GRACE and PERFORMANCE as the two routes, and LOVE at the base of the grace side. The righteousness James wants is *born of love*, not effort.

**Three beliefs that break the performance trap (p. 50).** Performance thinking survives because we half-believe each of these. Name all three; the trap is in the gaps.

1. **We lost it all.** All humanity lost all virtue in God's eyes — not just perfection, but all goodness. Isaiah's phrase: our performance-based righteousness is filthy rags. Performance says we lost *a lot, but not all* — that there's something good enough in us to trade.
2. **He did it all.** Christ did everything necessary to pardon lawbreakers. Nothing is left to be done; the great swap completely satisfies God. Performance says He did a lot, but we must add faith and repentance out of our own goodness. As the line goes: the only thing a person contributes to their salvation is the sin that made it necessary.
3. **We get it all.** God credits the full righteousness of Christ to His followers — forgiven of all past, present and future sin, fully assured of unconditional love, given everything needed to live rightly related and be satisfied. Performance says we get a lot, but not everything. Any gap left is the breeding ground.

**Where it bites (p. 50).** The diagnostics, unchanged from last week and worth reusing: do you look at past moral failures and wonder if they affect God's love for you? Do you find yourself going to church, giving, or doing personal worship because you worry God's favor won't be quite the same if you don't?

**Freedom is not license (Equipping, p. 51).** Galatians 5:1–8 — Christ set them free from the slavery of performance. Colossians 2:13–23 — what God has done frees us from the need to do for God. If a student lands on "so it doesn't matter what I do," that's James's exact concern, and the answer is love, not rules.
