# Song Exercise — Spotting the Act of Giving Glory

Handout or slides. Students mark every line that *overtly ascribes glory to God* — not lines about us, not petition, not testimony. The act itself.

**The point:** some songs do it head to toe. Some do it in a line or two. Knowing the difference is knowing what you're doing while you sing.

---

## Where it goes

**Week 1 (Sep 13) — one song, 4 min, as the hook.**
Holy, Holy, Holy on the screen. "Mark every line where we're giving God glory." Every line qualifies. That's the point. Then: what is that word? → Bible Hub → weight → Psalm 8.

**Week 4 (Oct 4, The Lord's Day) — full exercise, 10–12 min.**
Three or four songs in contrast. Whole-song, line-or-two, almost-none. Then the question: *what were you doing during the parts that weren't ascription?* That's the Lord's Day conversation.

## Head to toe — public domain

### Holy, Holy, Holy · Reginald Heber, 1826

Holy, holy, holy! Lord God Almighty!
Early in the morning our song shall rise to Thee;
Holy, holy, holy, merciful and mighty!
God in three Persons, blessed Trinity!

Holy, holy, holy! All the saints adore Thee,
Casting down their golden crowns around the glassy sea;
Cherubim and seraphim falling down before Thee,
Which wert, and art, and evermore shalt be.

Holy, holy, holy! Though the darkness hide Thee,
Though the eye of sinful man Thy glory may not see;
Only Thou art holy; there is none beside Thee,
Perfect in power, in love, and purity.

Holy, holy, holy! Lord God Almighty!
All Thy works shall praise Thy name, in earth, and sky, and sea;
Holy, holy, holy, merciful and mighty!
God in three Persons, blessed Trinity!

*Every line. Verse 2 is Revelation 4 — the elders casting crowns. Verse 3 has the word itself: "Thy glory."*

### Immortal, Invisible, God Only Wise · Walter Chalmers Smith, 1867

Immortal, invisible, God only wise,
In light inaccessible hid from our eyes,
Most blessed, most glorious, the Ancient of Days,
Almighty, victorious, Thy great name we praise.

Unresting, unhasting, and silent as light,
Nor wanting, nor wasting, Thou rulest in might;
Thy justice like mountains high soaring above
Thy clouds which are fountains of goodness and love.

To all, life Thou givest, to both great and small;
In all life Thou livest, the true life of all;
We blossom and flourish as leaves on the tree,
And wither and perish — but naught changeth Thee.

Great Father of glory, pure Father of light,
Thine angels adore Thee, all veiling their sight;
All praise we would render; O help us to see
'Tis only the splendor of light hideth Thee.

*Nearly every line is an attribute named to His face. The one exception is worth catching: verse 4 line 3 turns to petition — "O help us to see." Even a head-to-toe song has a seam.*

## A line or two — public domain

### Be Thou My Vision · Irish, tr. Mary Byrne 1905, vers. Eleanor Hull 1912

Be Thou my Vision, O Lord of my heart;
Naught be all else to me, save that Thou art;
Thou my best thought, by day or by night,
Waking or sleeping, Thy presence my light.

Be Thou my Wisdom, and Thou my true Word;
I ever with Thee and Thou with me, Lord;
Thou my great Father, I Thy true son;
Thou in me dwelling, and I with Thee one.

Riches I heed not, nor man's empty praise,
Thou mine inheritance, now and always:
Thou and Thou only, first in my heart,
High King of Heaven, my Treasure Thou art.

High King of Heaven, my victory won,
May I reach Heaven's joys, O bright Heaven's Sun!
Heart of my own heart, whatever befall,
Still be my Vision, O Ruler of all.

*Almost entirely petition — "be Thou." The ascription is in the names: "High King of Heaven," "O Ruler of all," "bright Heaven's Sun." Verse 3, line 1 is a direct hit on the counterfeits — "nor man's empty praise." Worth pointing at in week 1 if you use this one.*

## Almost none — public domain

### Amazing Grace · John Newton, 1779

Amazing grace! how sweet the sound,
That saved a wretch like me!
I once was lost, but now am found,
Was blind, but now I see.

'Twas grace that taught my heart to fear,
And grace my fears relieved;
How precious did that grace appear
The hour I first believed!

Through many dangers, toils, and snares,
I have already come;
'Tis grace hath brought me safe thus far,
And grace will lead me home.

The Lord has promised good to me,
His word my hope secures;
He will my shield and portion be,
As long as life endures.

*Testimony, first person, start to finish. God is spoken about, not to. The nearest thing to ascription is verse 4 — "He will my shield and portion be." Still not addressed to Him. Not a lesser song. A different act. That's the lesson.*

## Modern songs — pull lyrics from your app or CCLI

Titles and where the ascription sits. Not reproduced here.

**Head to toe**
- **God Omniscient, God All Knowing** (Boswell / Papa) — attribute after attribute, addressed to Him.
- **How Great Is Our God** (Tomlin) — the chorus is pure ascription; verses name attributes.
- **Holy Forever** (Wickham) — Revelation 4 again. Whole thing.
- **Great Are You Lord** (All Sons & Daughters) — ascription plus one line that names the act itself.

**A line or two**
- **10,000 Reasons** (Redman) — mostly exhortation to self. The ascription is in the bridge and a few verse lines.
- **Goodness of God** (Bethel) — testimony, like Amazing Grace, with ascription in the chorus.
- **Build My Life** (Housefires) — petition, with a stack of ascription lines in verse 1.
- **Living Hope** (Wickham) — narrative until the refrain.

**Almost none**
- **In Christ Alone** (Getty / Townend) — creed sung in first person. Overt ascription is rare; the song is confession, not address.

## The question at the end

*During the lines that weren't giving glory — what were you doing?*

Confessing. Asking. Remembering. Telling your own story. All of it is worship. Only some of it is ascription. Knowing which is which while you're singing is the whole skill.
